# Step 28 — an invoice with the wrong amount

R9 stands, and Tomek will not give it up: an issued invoice is never
changed, for anyone, and its number is never taken back or used again.
Jan only wants one action: he names the invoice and the amount that was
agreed, the customer gets the difference back, and the customer's papers
then say what they really paid. He does not care how the books show it. If
asked whether the amount on the invoice may simply be changed: no. The
system leaves the invoice as it is and makes a **credit note against it
for the difference**; the invoice and its credit notes together say the
agreed amount.

- "The amount of the invoice" that Jan corrects is what is left of it: its
  total minus the credit notes that are against it already (returns,
  earlier corrections). The amount agreed replaces that.
- **HD-156:** the correction is one credit note against that invoice, for
  the difference, in the credit notes' own series (`K/…`, HD-117), dated
  the day of the correction (the time the call is told to assume, or
  today; HD-118). It names the invoice and the order, and it carries the
  buyer as the invoice shows them. No goods came back: it lists the
  invoice's products with a quantity of zero.
- The difference goes back to the customer through the payment provider,
  against the order's charge, as a refund does.
- **HD-157, VAT (R7):** the difference is split over the goods on the
  invoice in proportion to what is left on each line (rounding as in
  HD-89); each share has the VAT rate of its line, its VAT is worked out
  from the share and rounded half up, and the credit note's total and VAT
  are the sums. Example: a mug at 12.30 € (23 %) and tea at 21.00 € (5 %),
  agreed 29.97 €: credit note 3.33 €, of which 1.23 € on the mug with
  0.23 € VAT and 2.10 € on the tea with 0.10 € VAT.
- **HD-158:** only downwards. An amount above what is left of the invoice
  is refused (there is no way to charge a customer a second time; a new
  invoice for more is not decided). An amount equal to what is left is
  fine and records nothing. An amount below zero or not in whole cents is
  refused; so is an invoice that does not exist. A credit note cannot be
  corrected. The shipping cost on an invoice is not corrected: an amount
  below it is refused.
- **HD-159:** when the refund fails at the payment provider nothing
  happens: no credit note, no number used, and the caller is told the
  provider is unavailable (as HD-116).
- **HD-160:** an invoice can be corrected more than once, and after a
  return; each time the amount agreed replaces what is left.
- **HD-161, returns afterwards:** the customer has now paid less for the
  goods, and a return never gives back more than they paid. Returns are
  worked on what was paid for the goods minus what corrections gave back;
  the return that completes the order gives back whatever is left. All
  refunds of an order, corrections included, add up to at most what was
  charged, and the credit notes of an order that was returned completely
  still add up to its invoices (without the shipping cost).
- **HD-162:** points follow the money (HD-50): after a correction the
  order keeps one point per full euro still paid for the goods; the rest
  is taken back by a record for that order (never more than the customer
  has, R5). Points that were used on the order do not come back.
- The correction changes no stock and no cost. It is on the daily sales
  report of its day as credited (HD-121), and its refund is a refund in
  the reconciliation.
- When the customer is erased, the buyer goes from this credit note like
  from any other (R4).

## Not decided

A reason on the correction, corrections upwards with a new invoice,
correcting the buyer's name or address on an invoice, who may correct.
