# Step 17 — VAT

## Standing requirement (it holds for the whole life of the system)

- **R7, Tomek:** money is whole cents, everywhere: no half cents are kept,
  shown or carried along. VAT is worked out per order line, never on a
  total: the amount of the line, the rate of the line, rounded half up to a
  whole cent (0.5 goes up). The VAT of an order is the sum of the VAT of
  its lines, and every total the system shows is the sum of the rounded
  figures below it: of the lines for an order (later for an invoice), of
  the documents for a report. A total is never worked out again from a
  percentage. Tomek's reason: the tax office adds up the lines, and a
  figure that is one cent off has to be explained.

## Categories and rates

- A category has a code, a name and a VAT rate. A product is in one
  category or in none. Tomek's categories today: tea 5 %, mugs, teapots and
  accessories 23 %, gift vouchers 0 %.
- **HD-86:** the rate is a whole number of percent, from 0 to 50; anything
  else is refused. The code is 2 to 20 characters: upper-case letters,
  digits and dashes, and unique. A product put into a category that does
  not exist is refused.
- A category's name and rate can be changed. Categories are not deleted.
- A product without a category carries no VAT, and neither does one in a
  category at 0 %. So everything that is in the system today stays as it
  is until Tomek has put the products into categories.
- **HD-90:** a product can be moved to another category, and taken out of
  its category again (then it carries no VAT).

## VAT on an order

- **HD-85:** the prices in the system include VAT, and they always have:
  they are what the customer pays. VAT changes no price and no total; it
  only says how much of the money is VAT. The VAT in an amount is
  amount × rate / (100 + rate). Example: a mug at 12.30 € in a 23 %
  category: the customer pays 12.30 €, of which 2.30 € is VAT.
- The order shows its VAT, and each line shows its rate and its VAT. The
  amount without VAT is the total minus the VAT; Tomek works that out.
- **HD-87:** the rate is fixed on the order when it is placed, as the price
  is. Changing a category's rate, or moving a product to another category,
  changes no order that exists; an order placed afterwards gets the new
  rate.
- **HD-88:** VAT is worked on what the customer really pays for the line:
  after the discount code and after loyalty points. Points count as a
  discount here, not as a way of paying. Example: the 12.30 € mug with a
  50 % code: 6.15 € paid, 1.15 € VAT.
- **HD-89:** the discount of a code, and what points take off, belong to
  the whole order, so they are split over the lines to get each line's
  amount: in proportion to the value of the lines, each share rounded down
  to a whole cent, and the cents left over go one each to the lines from
  the first line down. The amounts of the lines then add up exactly to the
  order's total (R7).
- Refunds for returns are worked out as before (HD-34, HD-56); VAT changes
  nothing in them. So are the points a customer collects (on what was
  paid, VAT included).

## Not decided

Different rates per destination country, customers with a VAT number,
prices shown without VAT, deleting a category.
