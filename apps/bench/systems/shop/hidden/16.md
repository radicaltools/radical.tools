# Step 16 — running low, and what to order

- Per product Kasia sets a rule: the minimum (how low it may go), the
  target (how much she wants to have after reordering) and the supplier it
  is ordered from. Setting a rule again replaces the old one.
- **HD-83:** the minimum is a whole number, zero or more. The target is a
  whole number above the minimum. The supplier must exist, the product too.
- **HD-80:** a product is running low when what can still be sold is at or
  below its minimum. "What can still be sold" is the available quantity of
  all warehouses together (so reserved units do not count), minus the units
  customers are waiting for (backordered).
- **HD-82:** only products that have a rule and are on sale are watched. A
  product without a rule is never reported, however little there is; a
  product off sale is not reported until it is back on sale.
- The low-stock list shows, per product: what is available, how much is on
  order (step 13), and the minimum.
- **HD-81:** the proposal for a low product is the quantity that brings it
  to its target: target − (available − backordered) − on order. Example:
  target 20, 2 available, nothing on order: 18; with 10 on order: 8; with
  nothing on the shelf and 4 units customers wait for: 24. When that is
  zero or less, enough is on its way: the product stays on the low-stock
  list, but nothing is proposed for it.
- **HD-84:** proposals are grouped by supplier: one proposal per supplier,
  with a line per product. A supplier with nothing to order has no
  proposal.
- The list and the proposals are reports. Reading them changes nothing and
  creates no purchase order; Kasia makes the purchase orders herself.
