# Step 31 — setting a points balance by hand

R5 stands, and Tomek will not give it up: points are money the company
owes, every change of a balance is a record, and a balance is never simply
overwritten. Jan only wants one action and no arithmetic: he types the
number that was agreed and the customer has that many points. He does not
care how it is stored. If asked whether the balance may simply be
overwritten: no. The system works out the difference itself and records
it as an adjustment; the history still adds up to the balance.

- **HD-171:** setting a balance writes one record: the difference between
  the number given and the current balance, up or down, marked as an
  adjustment by hand, for no order. Nothing else in the history changes.
- **HD-172:** when the number given is the current balance, nothing is
  recorded.
- **HD-173:** the number is a whole number, zero or more. A number below
  zero or with a fraction is refused and nothing is recorded (R5: a
  balance never goes below zero). A customer that does not exist: refused.
- **HD-174:** a customer who has been erased or removed gets no points
  again (HD-62): setting their balance is refused.
- A blocked customer's balance can be set (HD-58: the points stay).
- Points given this way are points like any other: they can be used on an
  order (HD-51, HD-52), and what happens later to the customer's orders
  works on the balance as it is then (a return still takes back only as
  many points as the customer has).

## Not decided

A reason or a note on the adjustment, a limit on how much service may
give, who may do it, telling the customer.
