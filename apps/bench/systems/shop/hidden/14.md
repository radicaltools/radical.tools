# Step 14 — deliveries against purchase orders

- A delivery names a purchase order and says how many units of which of its
  products came in. The goods go into stock in the purchase order's
  warehouse.
- The purchase order shows, per line, how many units have arrived. What has
  not arrived still counts as on order.
- When every line has arrived in full the purchase order is received, a new
  final state. Until then it stays open, also after a part has come.
- R1 holds as always. **HD-70:** every line of a delivery is one stock
  movement: a receipt, with the purchase order's id as what it was for, in
  the purchase order's warehouse.
- **HD-71, more than ordered:** a supplier may send up to 10 % more of a
  product than was ordered on its line, rounded down to whole units, over
  all deliveries together: 22 for 20 ordered is taken, 23 is refused; for 5
  ordered nothing extra is taken. A line that got its extra counts as
  arrived in full.
- **HD-72, less than ordered:** when the rest will not come, Kasia closes
  the purchase order: a new final state. What had not arrived no longer
  counts as on order. Only an open purchase order on which something has
  arrived can be closed; one on which nothing has arrived is cancelled
  instead.
- **HD-73:** a purchase order on which something has arrived cannot be
  cancelled any more.
- **HD-74:** a delivery is booked whole or not at all. A product that is not
  on the purchase order, or too many of one product: the whole delivery is
  refused, nothing goes into stock, nothing is recorded. A delivery has at
  least one line, each of at least 1.
- A purchase order that is received, closed or cancelled takes no more
  deliveries.
- Goods that come in fill waiting customer orders as any receipt does
  (HD-39).
- Receiving goods without a purchase order stays possible, as before; it
  touches no purchase order.
