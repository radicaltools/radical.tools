# Step 29 — a customer who wants to be gone

R4 stands, and neither Tomek nor the data protection consultant will give
it up: orders, payments, returns, invoices, credit notes and the stock
history are the company's books and are never deleted, whoever asks. The
law that lets the customer ask for erasure does not ask for that either,
and the tax office wants the invoices for years. R9 stands with it: no
invoice or credit note disappears and no number goes missing. What goes is
the person. Marta wants two things, and when she is told she agrees that
they are all she needs: **nothing about the woman is left anywhere in the
system**, and **it is done in one go**, without Jan having to clear the
customer's open orders first. If asked whether the orders may be deleted:
no. The orders stay, without the person's data.

- Removing a customer does what erasing does (step 12, HD-61 to HD-64):
  name, e-mail address, phone and addresses are gone from the account,
  from the orders (e-mail address and shipping address) and from the
  buyer on invoices and credit notes; the look-up by the old e-mail
  address finds nothing; the points are written off by a record (HD-62);
  the e-mail address is free for a new account, which is a new customer
  (HD-63). Everything else on the orders, payments, returns, invoices and
  credit notes stays as it was, and so does the stock history.
- What is new is that open orders no longer stand in the way (HD-60 stays
  for plain erasing):
  - **HD-163:** an order waiting for payment is cancelled, like any
    cancelled order: its stock is free again and the points it used come
    back (and are then written off with the rest).
  - **HD-164:** an order that is paid and of which nothing has gone out is
    called off: the customer gets all of its money back through the
    payment provider (the whole total, shipping cost included), its stock
    is free again, and it ends as cancelled. It never had an invoice, so
    there is no credit note. The payment and the refund stay on record.
  - **HD-165:** an order of which a part has gone out and the rest has not
    cannot be called off by the system: the removal is refused and nothing
    changes. Jan waits for the rest to go out, then removes the customer.
- **HD-166:** when the payment provider fails during a refund, the removal
  stops and the caller is told the provider is unavailable. The customer
  is still there with their data. Orders that were cancelled or called
  off before that stay so; doing it again goes on from there.
- **HD-167:** as after erasing, the account itself stays as an empty
  record: it has its id, it is marked as erased, name, e-mail address and
  phone are null, it has no addresses and no group, and it cannot order.
  The orders and the points history hang on that id; it says nothing
  about the person. Removing a customer who was erased or removed before
  is fine and changes nothing. A customer that does not exist: refused.
- Shipped and returned orders stay shipped and returned. Goods of a
  removed customer can still come back (HD-64).
- A blocked customer can be removed.

## Not decided

A confirmation sent to the customer, removing after a retention period,
removing customers who never had an account, an export of the data before
it goes.
