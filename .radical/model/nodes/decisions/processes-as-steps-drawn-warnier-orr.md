label: "Processes as steps, drawn Warnier–Orr"
alternatives: "Warnier as a drawing of sequences: flat, with no nesting, repetition or alternatives. Process logic as state machines: a machine is the states of one entity, not the steps of a procedure. Repetition and alternatives as properties of any element type: every type would carry fields that mean something only in a process. A separate process preset: models on the Radical metamodel would not get it. Activity diagrams (UML): a graph needing layout and more notation, where a Warnier tree is deterministic and reads like an outline."
consequences: "Process logic has a notation, verifiable by scenarios and traceable to requirements and to who performs each step, and it is written in the same picture it is read in. Every view can be read as a Warnier diagram, so any hierarchy gets one without new data. Every model on the unedited Radical metamodel gets the types and the viewpoint on its next load. Steps carry order; one without order comes after those with one, by name, until an edit in the view numbers it. Domain aggregates show no multiplicity yet: part-of has no cardinality."
context: "The product owner asked for Warnier–Orr diagrams, mainly for process logic and also for any hierarchy. The model has no place for a procedure: a sequence is a flat list of relation steps, a state machine the lifecycle of one entity, a scenario Given/When/Then text. Hierarchies it does hold (containment, a view's chain) are drawn as a canvas, a treemap, a table or a wiki. Warnier–Orr notation draws a hierarchy left to right in braces, with how often each part happens ((1,n), (0,1)), alternatives (⊕) and concurrency (+). The Markdown folder keeps no order among an element's children (files go by id)."
date: "2026-10-18"
decision: "The Radical metamodel gets a Process group with two node types: process (a procedure, at the root or in a system, domain or group) and step (inside a process or a step). A step has repeat (1, 0..1, 1..n, 0..n), a condition for it (\"for each order line\") and order, its place among its siblings; a process and a step have children: sequence (the default), choice (exactly one child runs) or concurrent. A step is performed-by a person, system, container, component or web app; a process satisfies requirements and scenarios verify it. A new view kind, warnier, draws what any view shows as a Warnier–Orr diagram: its chain as a tree, or with one level or none, the elements by containment, siblings by order; a state machine's states read with ⊕ for a compound state's children and + for a parallel state's regions. The viewpoint Process (Warnier) shows the processes and their steps. The tree is built in @radical/common (warnier.ts), so a later export or the CLI draws the same. The view edits like an outline where its tree is containment (add after or inside, indent, outdent, move, rename, repeat and children, delete; each edit renumbers the siblings' order and is one undo step); a tree of a chain of relations is renamed only, and added to in the table."
