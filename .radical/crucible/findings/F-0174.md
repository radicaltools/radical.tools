## Steps

1. Open the sample model, Core Banking, in Designer
2. Drag Access & Security by its header 30 px right, 20 px down (it is pinned)
3. Double-click Payments Service → Align children… → Keep in a row

## What differs

```text
Expected: FX Engine and Payment Validator go on one line, Payments Service and what they cover make room; Access & Security (pinned, unrelated) and Risk & Data keep their place and size
Actual: Access & Security (1516, 482) 520x568 -> (1544, 420) 520x630 while its children Auth Service and API Gateway stay; Risk & Data (500, 1160) 658x498 -> (528, 1098) 630x560, children unmoved; saved, kept after a reload. Without the pin the same refit of both groups. Each refit frame now holds its children 120 px below its top (Smart Layout's padding) instead of 58
Seen: 2 of 2 (and 3 more runs, with and without the pin)
Where it probably comes from: packages/layout/src/constraints.ts enforceAlignments: tidy() and the closing pass fit(pid) every parent with compoundPadding, fixed and non-carrying frames included; reached from diagramStore addAlignment -> _enforceLayoutConstraints -> viewInput applyAlignments, which passes no pins (guess). Same pattern as F-0138's fitAll
A test that would catch it: packages/layout/tests (constraints): enforceAlignments with a row inside container P and a sibling group G, pinned or not, whose children sit 58 px below its top: G's box is returned as given; plus packages/ui/tests/calmDrag.test.ts: addAlignment on Core Banking with dom-access pinned leaves dom-access and dom-risk at place and size
```

## Fixed (2026-11-15)

Lining up a row, column or grid fits only the frames round what it moved and pushes aside only what the members then cover, and Studio's and the MCP server's line-up keep pinned elements (and their frames) where they are, so a pinned or unrelated group keeps its place and size.
