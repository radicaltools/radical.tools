## Steps

1. add_node need 'Basket brief' (answer 'A basket holds at most 20 items and is kept for 30 days.' and a two-paragraph answer), requirement 'keep at most 25 items in a basket for 7 days', add_relation derives quoting the first answer (reports 2 findings: correct)
2. update_node: 25 -> 20
3. add_relation: a second requirement quotes the first paragraph of the two-paragraph answer
4. add_relation: a scenario verifies the requirement; update_node: it names 20; add_relation: a second scenario verifies it

## What differs

```text
Expected: A write that only takes findings away reports nothing brought in
Actual: 5 findings reported as brought in by 5 writes that only removed findings: 'does not say "30"' and 'says "7"' after fixing 25 -> 20; 'No requirement quotes 1 of the 2 parts' after quoting one part; 'takes "30" … its scenario does not say' after the scenario named 20; 'none of its 2 scenarios says' after adding a second scenario
Seen: 2 of 2
Where it probably comes from: apps/mcp/src/folderModel.ts wordsBroughtIn: findings are compared by rule, element and the whole message, and the messages list the values left, the parts left and the count of scenarios, so a finding that shrinks counts as new (a guess, from reading the code)
A test that would catch it: apps/mcp/src/folderModel.test.ts › requirements reviewed against their needs: after a write that reports two missing values, update_node correcting one of them reports no word finding; the same for quoting one part of a two-part answer and adding a second scenario
```

## Fixed (2026-11-15)

Word findings name the values or parts they are about, and the MCP server tells a finding only when it names one the model before did not, so a write that only takes some away tells none.
